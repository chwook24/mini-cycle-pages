Cycle prediction

How MiniCycle estimates periods, ovulation, and the fertile window

Published May 16, 2026 by MiniCycle

MiniCycle is a simple period calendar app for people who want fast, private cycle tracking without a heavy dashboard. The algorithm is intentionally modest: it uses your recent period records, applies research-informed cycle assumptions, and shows estimates as reference information rather than medical facts.

The short version

MiniCycle estimates your next period from the start dates you save. If you have enough records, it looks at recent cycle intervals and uses a median-based value. If you have not added enough history yet, it falls back to a default cycle length. It then estimates ovulation by counting backward from the next expected period, and marks the fertile window around that ovulation estimate.

Why recent records matter

Menstrual cycles are not perfectly fixed. Large real-world datasets show meaningful variation between people and across cycles. That is why MiniCycle does not treat a single old average as the whole story. It focuses on the most recent saved period starts, using up to the latest 12 records, so your current pattern has more influence than distant history.

The app also prefers a median-style estimate instead of a fragile average. When there are at least 10 measured cycle intervals, MiniCycle removes one shortest and one longest interval before calculating the median. This helps reduce the effect of unusual cycles caused by stress, illness, travel, medication changes, or missed logging.

How the next period is estimated

A cycle length is the number of days from one period start to the next period start. MiniCycle measures those intervals from your saved period ranges. If you manually set a cycle length, the app respects that setting. Otherwise, it estimates from recent records. If there is not enough history yet, MiniCycle uses 29 days as a practical default.

Period length is handled similarly. A manual setting takes priority. Without a manual setting, MiniCycle calculates the length of recent closed period ranges and falls back to 5 days when there is not enough data.

How ovulation is estimated

Ovulation is estimated by counting backward from the next expected period. This reflects a common cycle-tracking approach: the luteal phase, the phase after ovulation and before the next period, tends to be more constrained than the first part of the cycle.

MiniCycle uses a variable luteal phase estimate instead of a single fixed number. In the current app, the luteal phase is clamped between 9 and 14 days and adjusts with cycle length. This design was influenced by research on real-world cycle characteristics, including work by Bull and colleagues in npj Digital Medicine, which reported variation in cycle and luteal phase length across a very large dataset.

How the fertile window is shown

After estimating ovulation, MiniCycle marks the fertile window from 5 days before the estimated ovulation day through 1 day after it. This mirrors the practical idea that conception probability is concentrated in a limited window around ovulation. A classic prospective study by Wilcox and colleagues found that the fertile window spans the 5 days before ovulation and the day of ovulation. MiniCycle includes the following day as a conservative visual cue, because an app calendar should avoid implying day-level certainty.

Important: MiniCycle predictions are estimates for personal reference. They are not medical advice, contraception, fertility treatment, diagnosis, or pregnancy-related judgment. If cycle changes are sudden, painful, persistent, or clinically important, please talk with a qualified healthcare professional.

Why MiniCycle keeps the algorithm explainable

Some period tracker apps hide their predictions behind a complex score. MiniCycle takes the opposite approach. A simple period calendar should be understandable: recent records define the expected rhythm, ovulation is estimated from the expected next period, and the fertile window is shown around that estimate.

This also makes the product easier to use. You can open the app, check your calendar, and see the same rhythm in the home screen widget without creating an account or syncing sensitive records to a server.
